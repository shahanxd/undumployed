# Application Kit

> Build this once. Update it every 3 months. It unlocks most opportunities.

---

## The Standard Kit

| Document | Used For | Status |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **Resume / CV** | Everything | Build first |
| **Statement of Purpose (SoP)** | Research internships, fellowships, grad programs | Tailor per application |
| **Transcripts** | Almost all international programs | Get official + scan unofficial |
| **Letter of Recommendation (LOR)** | Research programs, fellowships | Ask 3+ months in advance |
| **NOC (No Objection Certificate)** | DRDO, ISRO, some IIT programs | From college admin |
| **GitHub profile** | All tech roles, GSoC, open source | Clean + pinned projects |
| **Portfolio** | Design / UX / architecture / arts roles and programs | Mandatory for design, see below |
| **Writing sample** | Policy, journalism, law, research fellowships (LAMP, PRS, YIF, think tanks) | 1–2 pieces, see below |
| **Media Kit** | Insider / creator programs | Optional but high-impact |

## Resume

**For research programs:**
- Use a 2-column academic CV format
- List research experience, publications, projects, courses, conferences
- Include CGPA, relevant coursework
- Europass format for European programs (ETH, EPFL, DAAD)

**For corporate roles:**
- 1 page maximum
- Reverse chronological (most recent first)
- Quantify everything: "Built X that reduced Y by Z%"
- No photo, no address beyond city

**Tools:** LaTeX (Overleaf), Canva, or a clean Word template. Avoid decorative templates.

**Resume templates worth using (free):**
- **Jake's Resume** (LaTeX) is the de-facto standard for Big Tech / quant / startup applications in India and the US. Single column, ATS-safe. [Overleaf template](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/jakes-resume/syzfjbzwjncs) · [GitHub source](https://github.com/jakegut/resume)
- **Overleaf CV gallery** for academic CVs (2-column "moderncv", "AltaCV", "Awesome CV" are all fine). [overleaf.com/gallery/tagged/cv](https://www.overleaf.com/gallery/tagged/cv)
- **Europass** is the EU standard; some German/French/Italian programs and DAAD forms ask for it explicitly. Build it online, export PDF. [europa.eu/europass](https://europa.eu/europass/en/create-europass-cv)
- **Harvard OCS / MIT CAPD formats**: the classic 1-page reverse-chronological Word/Google Doc templates that consulting and finance recruiters are used to. [Harvard resume & cover letter guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/) · [MIT CAPD resume resources](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/)
- Stick to one of these. Recruiters can spot a Canva "creative" template from across the room, and ATS parsers can't read it.

## Statement of Purpose (SoP)

**Structure that works:**
1. **Opening**: the specific problem or question you're interested in (not "I have always been passionate about...")
2. **Your background**: what you've done that's relevant. Specific, not generic.
3. **Why this program**: reference specific faculty, projects, labs, or features. Show you did your research.
4. **What you'll contribute**: concrete, not vague aspirations.
5. **What's next**: how this fits your longer-term direction.

**Length:** 1–2 pages. If a program specifies a word limit, follow it exactly.

**Common mistakes:**
- Generic openers about childhood curiosity
- No specific reference to the program's faculty or research
- Listing what the program offers without saying what you bring
- Passive voice throughout

## Letter of Recommendation (LOR)

**Ask early, at least 2–3 months before the deadline.**

**Who to ask:**
- A professor who has seen you work on a project or research
- A supervisor from an internship or research position
- Not a professor whose class you attended without other interaction

**What to give them:**
- Your CV / resume
- The program you're applying to and its deadline
- What you want them to highlight (specific project, skill, quality)
- A brief reminder of the work you did together

**Waive your right to view it**. Letters are more credible when waived.

## Transcripts

- Get an official sealed transcript from your university
- Keep 5–10 photocopies of unofficial transcripts scanned as PDF
- For programs requiring conversion to 4.0 scale: use WES (World Education Services) if required, or calculate using the program's stated conversion method

## NOC (No Objection Certificate)

Required for: DRDO, ISRO, some IIT programs, government labs.

**How to get one:**
1. Email your college's Academic/Dean office with the internship details
2. Specify: organization name, dates, nature of work
3. Get the letter on college letterhead with a seal

Allow 1–2 weeks processing time.

## GitHub Profile

Critical for: GSoC, LFX, Outreachy, all tech roles, research programs with a CS component.

**What makes a strong GitHub:**
- **Pinned repositories** showing your 4–6 best projects
- **README files**: each pinned project should have a clear README explaining what it does, why you built it, and how to run it
- **Commit history** with consistent contributions, not one giant commit
- **Contributions to open source**, even small bug fixes or documentation
- **Green squares** (the activity graph) matter less than quality, but showing consistent work helps

**Before applying:** Go through your profile as a stranger. Is it clear what you build?

## For Insider Programs: Media Kit

A simple 1-page PDF or link with:
- Your name and city
- Your platforms (Instagram/X/YouTube/blog) + follower counts
- Content samples (3–5 best posts or pieces)
- Contact email

Not required but high-impact for brand photowalks and insider applications.

## Portfolio for design students

For UX/product/communication/industrial design and architecture, the portfolio *is* the application. Resumes are skimmed; portfolios are read.

- **3–5 case studies, not 20 screens.** Each: problem → constraints → process (research, sketches, iterations, what you rejected and why) → final → outcome or what you'd do next. Recruiters at Google/Microsoft/Adobe/Figma-style teams look for reasoning, not polish.
- **One live link** (Behance, Dribbble, Notion, Framer, or a simple personal site) + a **PDF version ≤15 MB**. Many portals only accept PDFs.
- Put your best case study first and your name + role + contact on page 1. Password-protect NDA work rather than leaving it out.
- Competitions that double as portfolio pieces: Adobe Design Achievement Awards, D&AD New Blood, Red Dot Junior, Lexus Design Award India. See [Case, business & research competitions](../competitions/case-business-and-research.md).
- Free tools: Figma (education plan), Framer (free tier), Notion; see [Student perks](../student-perks/README.md).

## Writing sample for policy / journalism / law

Policy fellowships (LAMP, PRS, YIF, think-tank RA posts), journalism internships and many law/LLM applications ask for a **writing sample**. Keep two ready:

- **Analytical piece (800–1,500 words):** a policy brief, op-ed or short research note on one specific issue, like a bill, a court judgment or a dataset. Structure: the question → evidence → argument → a concrete recommendation. Cite sources. No "in today's fast-paced world" openers.
- **Reported / published piece (if you have one):** a campus-paper article, a Scroll/Wire/Print contribution, a Substack post with real reporting. Link + PDF.
- For law: a moot memorial or a case note (1,500–2,500 words) is the standard sample.
- Rules: the sample must be *solely yours*, recent (≤2 years), and match the program's word limit exactly. Follow the format the program specifies.
- Where these are asked: [Government & non-tech internships](../internships/government-and-non-tech.md), [Fellowships: India](../fellowships/india.md), [Study abroad](../fellowships/study-abroad.md).
