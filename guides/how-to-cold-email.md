# How to Cold Email a Professor

> For programs where you must secure faculty interest before applying: IIT Bombay, IISc, Max Planck, RIKEN, HKUST, Caltech SURF, Charpak Lab, and most rolling international programs.

---

## The core rule

**One paragraph. Specific. Not generic.**

Professors receive hundreds of emails from students copying the same template. The ones that get responses are short, show genuine knowledge of their work, and make it easy to say yes.

---

## Template That Works

```
Subject: Research Internship Inquiry — [Their Specific Research Area], [Your Season + Year]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I am a [year, e.g., 3rd year B.Tech] student in [field] at [your institution].
I came across your paper on [specific paper title or topic] and found [specific thing
that interested you — a method, a finding, a question it raised] particularly interesting
because [one sentence connecting it to what you're working on or thinking about].

I am applying to [program name, e.g., Caltech SURF / HKUST IVISP] and would be grateful
if you would be open to supervising a project related to [specific aspect of their research].
My background includes [1–2 relevant skills or experiences, not a full CV dump].

I have attached my CV and would be happy to share any additional information.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Institution] | [Email] | [LinkedIn/GitHub if relevant]
```

---

## What to customize every time

| Element | Do this |
|---------|---------|
| **Subject line** | Mention their specific research area. Never "Research Internship Request." |
| **Paper reference** | Reference a real paper of theirs. Not just their lab name or general topic. |
| **What interested you** | Be specific — a method, a result, a gap they identified. Not "your work is impressive." |
| **Connection to you** | One sentence on why *you* specifically care about this, based on your background. |
| **Project ask** | Be specific about what aspect of their work you'd want to contribute to. |
| **CV** | Attach a clean 1–2 page CV. Don't paste your entire resume in the email body. |

---

## What to avoid

- **Mass emails** — professors can tell. "I am interested in your research" with no specifics = delete.
- **Long emails** — if it's more than 5 sentences, cut it. They won't read it.
- **"I am highly motivated"** — every student says this. Show motivation through specifics, not words.
- **Asking for anything other than a conversation** — don't ask for a stipend, a visa letter, or a commitment in the first email.
- **Following up too soon** — wait 10–14 days before a polite follow-up. One follow-up maximum.

---

## Follow-up

If you don't hear back in 10–14 days:

```
Subject: Re: Research Internship Inquiry — [Their Research Area]

Dear Professor [Last Name],

I wanted to follow up on my previous email regarding a possible research internship.
I understand you are very busy — if you are not taking interns at this time, I completely
understand. Otherwise, I would be glad to share more about my background.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
```

One follow-up. If still no response, move to the next professor.

---

## What happens after they say yes

1. Thank them promptly
2. Ask about next steps — do they need anything from you for the formal application?
3. For programs with formal applications (Caltech SURF, Charpak), submit the formal application immediately and mention the professor's confirmation
4. Keep communication professional and brief until you're actually there
