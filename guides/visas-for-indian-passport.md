# Visas for an Indian Passport

> Which visa you need for a conference, hackathon final or research stay abroad, what it costs, and how early to start.

Rupee figures are rough conversions at ~₹85/US$, ~₹100/€ and ~₹115/£. Consulates change rules without notice, so re-check the official link in the last column before you pay.

- **USA:** The **Visa Integrity Fee US$250** (~₹21,000) was legislated in Jul 2025 for all nonimmigrant visas, collected at *issuance*, not at application ⚠️. Check on CGI Federal whether it is being charged before you pay. Interview waivers (dropbox) were sharply curtailed in late 2025, pushing renewals back into the queue. Social-media disclosure on DS-160 applies to all; the "public profile" requirement applies to F/M/J and other listed categories, not B1/B2 ⚠️.
- **Schengen:** **EES** (biometric entry/exit registration at the border) has been fully live since **10 Apr 2026** ✅. You give fingerprints and a face scan on first entry; there is no pre-registration. **ETIAS** (Q4 2026) does **not** apply to Indians because we need a visa anyway ✅. **Cascade rule for Indians (Apr 2024):** two Schengen visas lawfully used in the previous 3 years → 2-year multiple-entry visa; after that, a 5-year one (passport validity permitting) ✅ (German missions in India). It's discretionary: a consulate can still issue a shorter visa.
- **UAE:** ⚠️ Unclear whether a US *visit* visa still qualifies: GDRFA's own terms still say a US "visa or green card", and MEA's list (Feb 2026) also names valid visas from Canada, Australia, NZ, Japan, Singapore and South Korea. Confirm with your airline before you fly.

**Not covered here:** Brazil (e-visa since Apr 2025, low relevance), China (regular visa; long forms), Russia (e-visa 16 days exists), Latin America (see Section E for "valid US visa" shortcuts).

## B. Visas for internships and research stays

The tourist visa above is **not** the right visa if you're being paid, working on-site for more than a few weeks, or the host calls it an "internship". Hosts that run big programmes (CERN, ETH, EPFL, Mitacs, A*STAR, RIKEN, OIST, DAAD) know the drill and will send you the right paperwork; **your job is to reply fast and not miss their internal deadline**.

## C. Documents checklist

**Core (every visa):**
- Passport valid ≥6 months beyond return date, with ≥2 blank pages. Old passports too (travel history helps).
- Completed application form (DS-160 / gov.uk / VFS portal). Print the confirmation.
- Photos in the exact spec (US 2×2 in white background; Schengen 35×45 mm; UK now digital only via app in many cases). Get 8 printed; you'll need them.
- Cover letter: who you are, why you're going, dates, who is paying, and that you will return (one page, factual).
- Invitation / acceptance letter from the conference, host lab, hackathon organiser or company, on letterhead, with dates and who covers costs.
- **Bona fide student certificate / NOC from your college** stating you are enrolled, your leave dates, and that you are expected back (see [how to get an NOC](application-kit.md#noc-no-objection-certificate)).
- Flight *itinerary* (a reservation, not necessarily a paid ticket; Schengen and Japan accept reservations, so don't buy a non-refundable ticket before the visa).
- Accommodation proof: hotel booking or host's letter with address.
- Travel insurance (Schengen: mandatory ≥€30,000, all Schengen states; others: recommended).

**Financial (see Section F):**
- Last 3–6 months bank statements (yours, or the sponsoring parent's with a signed sponsorship letter + their ID + relationship proof).
- If a parent sponsors: their ITR (last 2 years), Form 16 or salary slips, and a one-line affidavit/letter of support.
- Scholarship/stipend letter if the programme funds you; this replaces most of the above.

**Programme-specific:**
- US J-1: DS-2019, SEVIS I-901 receipt, proof of medical insurance meeting J-1 minimums (host usually enrols you), proof of ties.
- France: signed convention de stage. Germany: hosting agreement / ZAV approval or exemption letter. Switzerland/Netherlands: cantonal/IND approval reference number.
- UK GAE / HK Training / Singapore TEP: the host's sponsorship reference. You can't apply without it.
- Conference travel: registration confirmation + paper acceptance email (if presenting) + any travel grant letter.

## D. "Book early" timelines

| Destination / purpose | Start the visa process | Why |
|---|---|---|
| USA B1/B2 (conference, hackathon final) | **9–12 months** before, or as soon as you know: interview slots, not paperwork, are the wait. Apply once you have a concrete trip (a conference you're submitting to, a summer school); the 10-year multiple-entry visa then covers the trips after it. | Delhi/Mumbai interview backlog 7–12 months in 2026 ⚠️. Expedited appointments exist for genuine emergencies and some conference presenters (embassy decides). |
| USA J-1 (summer research) | Day you receive DS-2019 (typically Feb–Apr for summer). Student/exchange slots come up faster than B1/B2 but still book same day. | SEVIS + DS-160 + interview = 4–8 weeks minimum. |
| Canada | **4–6 months** before travel. | IRCC's posted visitor-visa time for India was 32 days on 22 Sep 2026 ✅, but it swings; leave room for a refusal and a second try. |
| Schengen (summer) | **8–10 weeks** before; peak-season VFS slots vanish. You can apply up to 6 months before travel. | Slot scarcity in Apr–Jul. |
| UK | 6–8 weeks (max 3 months before travel). | 3-week standard, longer in peak. |
| Australia | 2–3 months. | 90% of 600 visitor visas are decided within 33 days, but some sit for 2–3 months. |
| Japan / Korea / Singapore / Taiwan visitor visa | 3–4 weeks. | Fast, but VFS slot + courier time. |
| Türkiye / Sri Lanka / Indonesia / HK PAR | 1 week. | Online and near-instant, but don't leave it to the airport. |
| Georgia / Vietnam e-visa | 2–3 weeks. | ~5 working days to process; Vietnam is slower around Tet. |
| Thailand TDAC / Malaysia MDAC | Within 3 days before arrival. | Arrival cards, not visas; MDAC can only be filed in the 3 days before you land. |
| Any *work-type* permit (Germany D, Swiss D, HK Training, Singapore TEP, UK GAE, Japan COE) | The day you accept the offer; expect **2–3 months** end-to-end. | Host-side approvals come first: MOM 2–4 weeks, cantonal 4–8 weeks, ImmD ~4 weeks, Japan COE 1–3 months (Section B). |

Also: **passport**. Tatkal takes ~1–3 weeks in Delhi if police verification is post-issue; normal is 4–8 weeks. Renew if <12 months validity: many countries want 6 months beyond your return, and the US visa is stamped for 10 years regardless of passport expiry (you carry both passports). [passportindia.gov.in](https://www.passportindia.gov.in/)

## E. The trick: one valid US, UK or Schengen visa unlocks a lot

A valid (in some cases even recently expired) visa from a "trusted" country makes many other countries visa-free, visa-on-arrival or e-visa-eligible for Indians. This is why getting a **10-year US B1/B2** early, and working up the Schengen cascade to a **2–5 year multiple-entry** visa (Section A), is worth the fee even if the first trip is small.

| With a valid… | You get | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US / UK / Canada / Australia / Japan / Korea / NZ / Schengen visa (valid, **or expired within 10 years**) | **Taiwan**: free TAC, 14 days/visit ✅ | Extended to 31 Dec 2027 ✅ |
| Schengen / US / UK / Ireland visa **or** residence permit (valid) | **Türkiye**: e-Visa, 1 month single entry ✅ | Sticker visa otherwise |
| EU / US / UK / Canada / Australia / Japan / Korea (and others) visa or residence permit | **Georgia**: visa-free 90 days ✅ | Else e-visa 30 days |
| US / UK / Schengen / Japan / Australia / Canada / Singapore visa | **Philippines**: extend 14 → 30 days ✅ | 14 days visa-free regardless |
| US / UK / Schengen / Canada / Australia / Japan visa or GCC residence | **Oman**: visa on arrival 14 days ✅ | |
| US / UK / Canada / Australia / NZ / Japan / Germany / Switzerland visa | **Singapore**: 96-hour Visa-Free Transit ✅ (transit only, third-country onward ticket) | Not for a Singapore-only trip |
| **US Green Card, or UK/EU residence *visa*** | **UAE**: visa on arrival 14 days ✅ | ⚠️ A valid US visit visa may also qualify (GDRFA's terms; MEA's Feb 2026 list adds Canada, Australia, NZ, Japan, Singapore, South Korea), but GDRFA's summary omits it. Confirm with your airline |
| US visa (valid) | **Mexico** (180 days), **Panama**, **Costa Rica**, **Colombia**, **Peru** (also with UK/Canada/Schengen/Australia), **Guatemala**, **El Salvador**, **Honduras**, **Dominican Republic** ⚠️ | Rules change often; check each country's consulate before you book |
| US / UK / Schengen visa | **Montenegro, Albania, North Macedonia, Bosnia** (typically 15–30 days) ⚠️ | Balkan states have changed rules often; verify before you book |
| Any Schengen visa | **Bulgaria & Romania**, same as the rest of Schengen ✅ | Full Schengen members since 1 Jan 2025 |

**Order of operations if you are starting from zero:** Sri Lanka / Thailand / Malaysia / Nepal (free, builds "travel history") → Japan or Schengen (cheap, fast, real visa) → UK → US (long wait, apply in parallel) → Canada last.

## F. Proof-of-funds norms

Nobody publishes a magic number for visitor visas, but officers expect the balance to cover the trip *and* look like it belongs to you or your sponsor.

| Country | What officers look for (visitor / short stay) | Sponsor allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| USA (B1/B2) | No amount. The interview is about **ties to India** and whether the trip makes sense for a student; funding usually parents/programme. Carry proof but they rarely read it. | Yes: parents or the inviting organisation. |
| UK | 3–6 months statements; cost of trip clearly affordable; **no unexplained large deposits in the last 3 months** (top refusal reason). Salary credits should match ITR/salary slips. | Yes: parents with proof of relationship + their statements, or the host organisation (letter must say what it covers). |
| Schengen | Means of subsistence vary by country. Germany's missions in India say there are no fixed daily rates; it depends on the type and length of stay ✅. Indicative figures elsewhere: France ~€65/day with a paid hotel, more without; Spain ~€120/day with a minimum ~€1,065; Italy varies by stay length ⚠️. Plus insurance. Programme stipend letter satisfies this. | Yes: sponsorship letter + sponsor's statements; Germany accepts *Verpflichtungserklärung* from a resident; France *attestation d'accueil* lowers the daily figure. |
| Japan | Bank statement showing roughly ₹1.5–3 lakh available for a 1–2 week trip is the informal norm ⚠️ (VFS/agent practice, not an official figure). | Yes: parent's statement + letter. |
| Canada | No figure for visitors; expect them to check that funds are consistent with your family's income and that you have "strong ties" (enrolment, family, property). | Yes. |
| Australia | No figure; "genuine temporary entrant" reasoning. Enrolment letter + parent's funds is standard for students. | Yes. |
| Singapore / Korea / Taiwan | Last 3–6 months statements; ~₹1–1.5 lakh balance is comfortably enough for a short trip ⚠️. | Yes. |
| Internships / J-1 / D visas | Stipend letter is primary. If the stipend is below the country's living threshold (Germany €992/month ✅, Switzerland cantonal minimums, US J-1 sponsor minimums), you top up with a blocked account / parent's guarantee. | Yes, and host letters count. |

Practical: keep one savings account with 6 months of clean history, no cash lumps in the last 90 days, and a parent's account you can *also* show. If a relative transfers money for the trip, do it 3+ months before applying and keep the gift letter.

## G. Common rejection reasons for students

1. **"Insufficient ties" / 214(b) (US), "not satisfied you will leave" (UK/Canada)**: you are young, single, no property, no job. Counter: bona fide student letter with return date, enrolment for next semester, family in India, a *specific* plan (conference dates, return flight).
2. **Money that doesn't add up**: a ₹5 lakh deposit a week before applying; parent's income ₹4 lakh/year and a ₹6 lakh balance with no explanation; sponsor with no proof of relationship. Counter: 6 months of statements, ITR, and a one-paragraph explanation of any lump sum.
3. **Purpose unclear**: no invitation, an invitation that doesn't mention who pays, or a "tourism" application when you're actually going to work in a lab. Counter: the host's letter must name you, the dates, the activity and who pays.
4. **Fake or cancellable bookings**: Schengen consulates do check hotel reservations; cancelled-after-submission bookings are a known refusal trigger.
5. **Wrong visa category**: applying for a tourist visa for a paid internship, or B1/B2 when you need J-1. Refusal and a record.
6. **Inconsistencies**: different dates across cover letter, invitation and itinerary; DS-160 answers not matching your interview answers; undisclosed previous refusals (UK and Canada treat that as deception).
7. **Passport validity / blank pages**: rejected at the VFS counter.
8. **Insurance gaps (Schengen)**: cover below €30,000, or not covering all Schengen states, or dates not covering the whole stay.
9. **Social-media inconsistencies (US F/M/J)**: handles omitted from DS-160, or private profiles when the category requires public ✅.
10. **Timing**: applying so late that the consulate cannot decide before travel (and then withdrawing, which also goes on record).

If refused: read the refusal letter (UK/Schengen/Canada give reasons; US gives a section number). Fix the *specific* reason and reapply; re-submitting the same file is the most common second refusal.
