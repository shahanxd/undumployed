## The big paid ones

### Google Summer of Code (GSoC)
<!-- tags: stage=ug,pg,phd,grad; field=cs; region=remote; funding=stipend; citizenship=any; gates=age-limit; format=remote -->
> **Type:** Paid open-source contribution program | **Location:** Remote | **Funded:** ✅ $750 (small) / $1,500 (medium) / $3,000 (large) for India; global caps $1,650 / $3,300 / $6,600

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Deadline | Contributor apps **~Mar 16–31, 2027** (2026 cycle: Mar 16–31). Org apps ~Jan 19–Feb 3; accepted orgs announced ~Feb 19. 2027 dates not yet published, so these are pattern-based. |
| Duration | 12 weeks standard (late May–late Aug), extendable to ~22 weeks (through early Nov) |
| Who | 18+ at registration; a student, or an open-source beginner if you are not a student. Any nationality, but residents of US-embargoed countries are excluded. |
| Stipend | PPP-adjusted by country of residence. India sits at the floor: $750 / $1,500 / $3,000. Paid 45% after midterm, 55% at end. |
| Stage | UG / PG / PhD / recent grads |

**What it is:** Google pays you to ship a scoped project (90 / 175 / 350 hours) inside a real open-source org, such as Apache, PSF, GNOME, CERN-HSF, KDE, Wikimedia, Blender, Godot, PyTorch-adjacent orgs and dozens of science and ML orgs. 2026: 15,245 applicants, 23,371 proposals, 1,141 accepted across 184 orgs (~7.5% by applicant).

**Reality check:** The proposal is graded against your pre-application contributions to that org. Showing up in March with no merged PRs is a near-certain rejection. Most Indian selections come from people who started in Nov–Jan. Pick 1–2 orgs, not 10. See the [6-month plan](#how-to-actually-get-into-gsoc-the-6-month-plan) below.

**Apply:** [summerofcode.withgoogle.com](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com) · [stipend table](https://developers.google.com/open-source/gsoc/help/student-stipends) · [timeline](https://developers.google.com/open-source/gsoc/timeline)

### Outreachy
<!-- tags: stage=ug,pg,phd,grad; field=cs; region=remote; funding=stipend; citizenship=any; gates=diversity,age-limit; format=remote -->
> **Type:** Paid remote internship | **Location:** Remote | **Funded:** ✅ $7,000 for 13 weeks + $500 travel stipend (when available)

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Deadline | **Dec 2026 cohort:** initial apps closed Aug 31, 2026; contribution period Oct 5–Nov 2, 2026; interns announced Nov 30; internship Dec 7, 2026–Mar 8, 2027. **May 2027 cohort:** initial apps `~early Feb 2027` (usually a 1–2 week window), contribution period ~Mar–Apr. |
| Duration | 13 weeks, 30 hrs/week |
| Who | 18+; people subject to systemic bias/underrepresentation in tech in their country (in India this includes women, caste-oppressed groups, LGBTQIA+, disabled applicants, and others; you write an essay). Not past Outreachy or GSoC interns. Cannot hold a full-time job or another internship during the term. |
| Stipend | $7,000, paid in installments |
| Stage | UG / PG / PhD / non-students |

**What it is:** Two cohorts a year (May, December). You pass an essay-based initial application, then spend a month making recorded contributions to 1–2 projects (GNOME, Debian, Mozilla, Wikimedia, Fedora, Linux kernel, scientific Python, and many more). Mentors pick interns from those who contributed.

**Reality check:** The initial application is the real gate: the essays about the bias you face are read seriously, and generic answers get rejected. University students must have 42 consecutive days free of classes and exams inside the internship dates, judged from the official academic calendar (taking a term off does not count). Most Indian semesters fail that test for the December cohort and pass it for May, so students should aim for May.

**Apply:** [outreachy.org/docs/applicant](https://www.outreachy.org/docs/applicant/)

### MLH Fellowship
<!-- tags: stage=ug,pg,grad; field=cs; region=remote; funding=stipend; citizenship=any; gates=age-limit; format=remote -->
> **Type:** Remote fellowship (structured, pod-based) | **Location:** Remote | **Funded:** ✅ "Educational stipend", amount not published on the site; historically up to $5,000 for 12 weeks, need- and region-based

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Deadline | Fall 2026 batch closed Aug 31, 2026 (runs Sep 14–Dec 4). **Spring 2027 batch:** apply `~Oct–Dec 2026`, rolling until pods fill; batch runs ~Jan–Apr 2027. Summer 2027: apps ~Feb–Apr 2027. |

| Who | 18+; any country; students or recent grads. Tracks currently listed: Software Engineering, Production Engineering (SRE), Web3 Engineering. |

**Reality check:** Admission is rolling until pods fill, so apply early in the window rather than near the end. Selection is a coding challenge plus an interview.

**Apply:** [fellowship.mlh.com](https://fellowship.mlh.com/)

## Linux Foundation family

### LFX Mentorship
<!-- tags: stage=ug,pg,phd,grad; field=cs; region=remote; funding=stipend; citizenship=any; gates=age-limit; format=remote -->
> **Type:** Paid mentorship (CNCF, LF AI, Hyperledger/LFDT, OpenSSF, kernel, and dozens of LF projects) | **Location:** Remote | **Funded:** ✅ $3,000–6,600 by country of residence (India is in the lower tier), paid in tranches

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Deadline | Three 12-week terms a year. **Fall 2026 (Term 3):** apps were Aug 3–18, 2026, now closed; term runs Sep 7–Nov 27, 2026. **Spring 2027 (Term 1):** apps `~late Jan–early Feb 2027`, term ~Mar–May. **Summer 2027 (Term 2):** apps `~May 2027`. |
| Duration | 12 weeks |
| Who | 18+, any country. Each project sets its own bar; CNCF projects (Kubernetes, Prometheus, Envoy…) want Go and prior issues/PRs. |
| Stipend | Tiered by country; most Indian mentees report ~$3,000 for a full-time term |
| Stage | UG / PG / PhD / early-career |

**What it is:** Each term, projects post specific mentorship tasks on the LFX platform; you apply per-project with a CV + cover letter (you can apply to several). Mentors review your prerequisite tasks (usually a small PR) and pick.

**Reality check:** Less crowded than GSoC per seat, but CNCF seats get hundreds of applicants each. The prerequisite task is where you're judged. Apply to 3–5 projects, not one. Also the umbrella for [Linux Kernel Mentorship](#linux-kernel-mentorship-program) and [Open Mainframe](#open-mainframe-project-mentorship) below.

**Apply:** [mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org](https://mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org/) · [CNCF timelines](https://github.com/cncf/mentoring/blob/main/programs/lfx-mentorship/README.md)

### Linux Kernel Mentorship Program
<!-- tags: stage=ug,pg,phd,grad; field=cs; region=remote; funding=stipend; citizenship=any; gates=age-limit; format=remote -->
> **Type:** Paid mentorship (kernel bug-fixing / development) | **Location:** Remote | **Funded:** ✅ LFX stipend (by country)

| Field | Details |
|-------|---------|
| Deadline | Runs on the LFX calendar: Spring (apps `~Jan–Feb 2027`), Summer (`~May 2027`), Fall (`~Aug 2027`) |
| Duration | 12 weeks full-time (or 6-month part-time variants some terms) |
| Who | 18+; solid C; you must complete the kernel "prerequisite tasks" (build the kernel, submit a cleanup patch to staging) before selection |
| Stipend | LFX tiered stipend |
| Stage | UG / PG / PhD |

**What it is:** Shuah Khan's long-running program: bug-fixing mentorships (syzbot bugs, KUnit tests, driver cleanups) that turn into upstream commits under your name. Strongest possible signal for systems/embedded roles.

**Reality check:** The prerequisite patch filters most applicants; if you've never used `git send-email`, start two months early. Read the mailing-list etiquette or you'll be ignored.

**Apply:** [wiki.linuxfoundation.org/lkmp](https://wiki.linuxfoundation.org/lkmp) → then LFX platform

## How to actually get into GSoC: the 6-month plan

**Oct–Nov 2026: pick the org.** Open the [2026 org list](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/programs/2026/organizations) (most orgs return). Filter by a language you already write. Shortlist 3, pick 2. Read their "ideas list" from last year and their contributor guide. Join their Matrix/Discord/mailing list and lurk.

**Dec 2026: first PR.** Build the project from source. Fix a `good-first-issue`. Get it merged. Then a second. If you skipped Hacktoberfest, [Season of KDE](#season-of-kde) and [KWoC](#winter-of-code-programs-india) are natural December on-ramps.

**Jan 2027: become known.** Aim for 3–5 merged PRs, at least one non-trivial (a bug in core logic, not a typo). Review someone else's PR. Ask mentors what they want next summer; several orgs pin "GSoC 2027 ideas" threads in January.

**~Feb 19, 2027: org list drops.** Confirm your org made it. If not, your PRs still count at a sister org (same umbrella, same ecosystem). Draft your proposal in the org's template now, not in March.

**Mar 1–15, 2027: draft review.** Share your draft with the mentor on the org channel. Iterate twice. A proposal with a mentor's fingerprints on it is what gets selected.

**~Mar 16–31, 2027: submit.** Max 3 proposals; realistically one strong one per org. Include links to every merged PR. Then keep contributing until results come out (~Apr 30). Mentors watch.

Fallbacks if you miss: [LFX Summer term](#lfx-mentorship) (May), [OSPP](#open-source-promotion-plan-ospp) (May–Jun), [C4GT DMP](#code-for-govtech-c4gt-dedicated-mentoring-program) (Apr–May), [Outreachy Dec cohort](#outreachy) (Aug; mainly for non-students, since students need 42 consecutive free days between Dec and Mar).

## Open-source as an employer signal

- A **merged upstream PR in a project the interviewer has heard of** (Kubernetes, CPython, PyTorch, LLVM, Chromium, the kernel) beats any certificate. Link the PR, not the program.
- **GSoC / Outreachy / LFX / Summer of Bitcoin** are names most tech recruiters recognise. GSSoC/Winter of Code are not. List the contribution, not the badge.
- Maintainers hire. Many mentors work at the company behind the project, and strong mentees get **direct offers** from it. Say yes to the "want to keep working with us?" email.
- Keep your GitHub contribution graph honest: reviewers can tell a 300-commit README-farm from a 12-commit real contributor in ten seconds.
